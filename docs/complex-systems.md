# Complex Systems

A complex system contains interacting components whose combined behaviour cannot be understood reliably by inspecting each component in isolation.

Examples include:

- an electronic order book
- a fusion plasma and its control environment
- an industrial production line
- a patient connected to multiple monitors
- a distributed computing network
- an autonomous platform with multiple sensors

## Why relationships matter

Suppose temperature, pressure and vibration all remain within their normal individual ranges. A fault may still be developing if the usual relationship between pressure and vibration breaks down.

That change is structural. It concerns the system's joint behaviour.

## Operating regimes

A regime is a period in which the system follows a recognisable pattern of behaviour. Regimes can be:

- normal operation
- degraded operation
- transition
- disturbance
- recovery
- previously unseen behaviour

Regime boundaries may be abrupt or gradual.

## Evaluation implication

A benchmark for a complex system should include changes in relationships, not only isolated spikes. Otherwise it tests ordinary anomaly detection rather than system-state inference.
